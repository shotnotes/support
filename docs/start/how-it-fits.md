# How it fits together

Shot Notes has two halves that do one job. A manual lens tells the camera
nothing, so the camera's file has no aperture, no focal length and no lens
name. Shot Notes keeps those on your phone, then puts them into the photos.

1. **Note** each shot with **the app**, on your phone, as you take it.
2. **Export** the notes. The app saves them in a file, and you send the file to
   your computer.
3. **Write** the notes into the photos with **the metadata writer**, on your
   computer.

## What you need

| You want to | You need |
|---|---|
| Note shots | The app, on an iPhone or an Android phone. |
| Put the notes into the photos | The metadata writer, on a Mac, a Windows PC or a Linux computer: the [desktop app](../writer/desktop-app.md), the [command-line tool](../writer/command-line.md), or both. |
| Do the same from inside Lightroom Classic | The [Lightroom plugin](../writer/lightroom.md), which uses the command-line tool. |
| Write the notes at all | [exiftool](https://exiftool.org/), a free program that the writer uses to change the files. |

You can start with the app alone. It keeps your shots, and you can search them
and look at them, with no computer. The writer is for when you have photos to
put the notes into.

Nothing needs an account. The notes stay on your phone until you export them.
